# Independent review: stable-profit plan (hyperliquid-ic)

Read-only. Where I quote numbers not in the saved summaries, they come from quick in-memory queries of `_artifacts-rewrite/{features,labels,predictions-short-core}.parquet` over the headline year (335 dates with a 30-day label), equal-weighted per row, hold-to-horizon, overlapping labels, in-sample. They are directional diagnostics, not results.

## 1. Diagnosis

### Strong findings (replicated across both tracks or directly verifiable)

1. **Risk persists; return does not.** Trailing interval/daily vol → forward variance IC ≈ 0.77 (IC track); `inverse_vol` → forward vol Spearman 0.70 with a simultaneous lower bound 0.55 (NB28). Growth IC for the short-core Ridge is 0.12 at 30d and a plain low-downside sort beats it at every horizon.
2. **The growth "signal" is loser-avoidance, not winner-picking.** Per-date quintiles of `interval_downside_dev_30` (zero-downside = 0): rank IC over all rows 0.151; excluding the highest-downside quintile 0.077; within the lowest three quintiles 0.017; within the lowest two −0.005. The top-downside quintile has a 47% rate of >10% 30-day loss versus 6–18% elsewhere. All the information is in the tail. Low-downside quintile: median forward growth 0.000 (52% of it is zero-downside rows), mean −28% annualised.
3. **The universe is a graveyard.** Mean forward 30-day log growth is negative in every TVL bucket except >$10M (n=1,085, +18%, 0% loss rate — the HLP-type names). Medians are ≈0. A long-only basket earns only from a minority of names, and those names are volatile: within the incumbent's top-12 neighbourhood, the high-vol tercile has both the highest blow-up rate (30% vs 10%) and the big winners.
4. **Low-risk selection lowers volatility and kills return, everywhere it has been tried.** NB29 dose-response (engine harness): q 0.10→0.50 takes CAGR 38.9%→1.8%, Sharpe 2.35→0.34, vol 0.154→0.057. A proxy of the plan's A1 (positive 30-day growth, rank ascending downside, top-6) on the IC panel: 37% zero-downside names, median forward growth +0.1% annualised, mean −5%. The incumbent proxy on the same rows: median +18%, mean −17%, 18.6% blow-up rate. Neither is "stable profit".
5. **Nothing predicts event concentration / spikiness** (NB28: all 13 signals fail on `forward_event_top5`; both concentration signals have the wrong sign on forward vol). The plan's Phase 2 "repeatability" family is a re-test of this.
6. **Production is a de facto 360-day strategy.** `cagr_sortino_weight` is NaN → 0 below 360 days of carried candles; NB23 measured 72% of gated reads unscored and strict admission bit-identical; in the replay 0.4% of incumbent top-6 slots have <360 days of history. Young vaults do not compete unless fewer than six scored names exist (3 of 257 dates).
7. **Window resolution.** ~129 active rebalances; 108 zero-mark dates; Sharpe differences between arms are unresolvable at this length (NB03a MDE ≈ 2.5 on 125 cycles). Any shortlisting rule keyed on "higher Sharpe" is keyed on noise.

### Hypotheses (consistent sign, not established)

- **Directional exposure is the blow-up axis the user wants to avoid.** Within the incumbent's top-12 neighbourhood (2,350 rows, 200 dates with both features), a per-date double sort of `interval_vol_30` × |`btc_beta_30`|: the low-|β| column has loss rates 2.8% / 6.1% / 11.5% across vol terciles; the high-vol × high-|β| cell (544 rows, the largest high-vol cell) has a 30% loss rate and median −7% annualised. High-|β| loses more often in 7 of 8 months; it earns more only in trend months (March: median +63%). |β| and vol correlate 0.76, so most — not all — of this is vol.
- **The 45-day Sortino leg is anti-predictive at the selection margin.** Within the same neighbourhood `daily_sortino_45` has daily rank IC −0.109 (negative in 5 of 6 measurable months; raw t −4.4, unadjusted for overlap). NB23's leg swap failed, but it swapped Sortino for another Sortino; nobody has tested weight 1.0/0.6 or the inverted leg in this harness.
- **Young cohorts are a loss-avoidance problem first.** Among gate-passing fresh rows, 14–29-day names have a 28.5% blow-up rate and mean −127% annualised versus 14.3% / −33% for 360+. "Usable IC" in those cohorts is not "profitable access".

### Artefacts to keep in view

- **Composite saturation.** Median incumbent top-6 score 0.999, 75th percentile 1.0; 58% of picks have trailing 360-day growth ≥100%. On 44 of 256 dates six or more candidates score exactly 1.0 and address order decides the basket; ties straddle the sixth slot on 22 dates. Any "rank by production composite" step inherits this.
- **Stale marking flatters sparse books.** Stale holdings carry last NAV; arms with more stale positions (incumbent 2.0, fixed 3.2, adaptive 6.1) get mechanically lower marked vol/DD. Portfolio risk comparisons across arms are confounded unless made on a coarse common clock.
- **Risk-model coverage.** Dense daily-loss labels exist only from mid-January 2026, so the forward-downside Ridge has predictions on 135 of 365 dates (from 1 May 2026). Weekly vaults never have a daily-risk label; growth labels (interval endpoints) exist for them.
- **Sharpe in `summarise_backtest` has no yield reference**, so a near-cash yield basket scores well on every plan criterion except the aspiration.
- The one BTC regime (115.9k → 77.3k over the year) colours every β-related result.

## 2. Three experiments of my own

I agree with the "minimal incremental change to hyper-ai" framing. The evidence says the incumbent's return engine is the high-CAGR names and any replacement ranking that targets low risk picks non-earners. What the evidence does *not* support is 20–30% CAGR *and* stability on this universe: the stable subset earns a median 5–15% annualised; the 20–30% comes from the directional/volatile names. That trade-off should be stated to the user as a finding, not hidden in an aspiration line.

| # | Idea | Mechanism | Cheapest discriminating experiment | Why it could fail |
|---|---|---|---|---|

## 4. Minimal execution order

1. **Selection diagnostics on saved artefacts, no new model, no replay** (one notebook, hours): incumbent top-6/top-12 proxy tables; tail decomposition; vol × |β| double sort with interval-aligned β; Sortino-leg IC by month; the A1/A2-proxy tables. This decides which arms get a replay — on current evidence A1 as written does not.
2. **Refactor the replay into pool/rank/size stages; A0 bit-for-bit parity; add coarse-clock metrics and stale-capital share.**
3. **Replay at H=30 only:** A0; A0 + |β| veto (idea 1); A0 + top-quintile downside veto (A3 corrected); A1 corrected (growth-ranked within low-risk half). Four runs, one of them the anchor.
4. **Sortino-leg sign test** (idea 2): two extra replays on the parity-checked path.
5. **Phase 3 sizing sensitivity** (no cap redistribution, cash-matched) on A0 and the single best step-3 candidate.

**Drop:** Phase 2 repeatability family as a model input (keep a one-cell IC check). **Defer:** A2 predicted-downside ranking (coverage from May only — run as a diagnostic on May–Sep if at all); H=14/45 sensitivities until one candidate survives; young-vault access extension until step 1 shows a young cohort with a blow-up rate no worse than 360+ (keep the 5% cap when it runs); BTC-up/down conditional growth features (one regime; report the split instead).

## 5. Verdict

**Not executable as written; executable after the four blocking corrections and the A3/A1 redefinitions.** The plan is honest about caveats and correctly preserves the data contract, but its primary arms are built on the low-risk-ranking premise that both tracks have already falsified, one of them (A2/A3) cannot run on two-thirds of the year, its parity test cannot be implemented on the current code path, and its shortlist criteria are confounded by stale marking and an unreferenced Sharpe. Corrected as above it becomes a cheap, bounded test of the only mechanism the evidence still supports: keep the incumbent's return engine and veto the directional/tail-risk names — while telling the user that "stable" and "20–30%" are, on this universe and this year, different baskets.
