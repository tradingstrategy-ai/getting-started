## Objective

Find information in vault histories that predicts **positive, repeatable returns with lower future risk**, then determine whether that information can improve portfolio allocation. Accept lower returns than the incumbent in exchange for a steadier equity curve. The working economic objective is 20–30% annual CAGR; this is a hypothesis to test, not a forecast or a requirement to maximise returns above 30%.

Young vaults must be able to qualify on the evidence they actually have. Missing 360-day history must not automatically exclude a vault, turn its score into zero, or count as poor performance. Equally, a short smooth history must not receive the confidence of a long, well-observed history.

[Stratwise Multi-Asset Public](https://tradingstrategy.ai/vaults/stratwise-multi-asset-public) is an illustrative case, not a training label or a hand-picked allocation. On 13 September the page reports data beginning 16 July 2026, roughly 0.2 years of age, 24.4% annualised net return and 3.9% volatility in its three-month display. The actual history is shorter than three months. Its fee mode is externalised. Use the underlying timestamped history to examine it; do not copy the website's current metrics into historical features. An apparently algorithmic equity curve is a measurable price behaviour, not proof of the manager's trading process.

Allowed predictive inputs: vault share price, timestamped vault age, historical TVL, and Binance BTC price/volatility. Source freshness and missingness are measurement controls. Exclude follower counts, leader activity, deposits by followers, social/strategy descriptions and other Hyperliquid metadata factors. Operational deposit/redemption restrictions and fees remain necessary execution inputs.

## Phase 2: define forward outcomes before ranking features

Measure future return and future risk separately so a feature cannot win merely by selecting nearly inactive or steadily losing vaults. Avoid a freely tuned “stable CAGR” composite label.

| Outcome | Horizon | Definition and role |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Forward log growth | 7, 14, 21, 30, 45, 60, 90 days | Primary return forecast: `g = log(P_exit / P_entry)`. Estimate expected log growth conditional on information at entry; keep its magnitude as well as its cross-sectional rank. |
| Forward downside semivariance | 7, 14, 21, 30, 45, 60, 90 days | Primary risk forecast: `d = mean(min(r,0)^2)` over future daily returns, relative to a zero minimum acceptable daily return. Model on the squared-risk scale; display downside deviation as `sqrt(365*d)`. All valid daily observations enter the mean, not only negative observations. |
| Forward total variance | 7, 14, 21, 30, 45, 60, 90 days | Mandatory companion risk forecast/comparison: sample variance `v = sum((r-mean(r))^2)/(n-1)`. Display annualised volatility as `sqrt(365*v)`. This exposes upward-jump volatility that downside risk alone misses. Use the same frozen feature shortlist and folds; do not launch a separate search for a third winning feature family. |
| Forward Sharpe and Sortino | All seven horizons; 90 days is the main persistence diagnostic | Descriptive quality outcomes for shortlisted features, not initial model-fitting targets. Short-horizon ratios are explicitly labelled noisy; report missing denominators and negative-day counts. Use zero risk-free/minimum acceptable return initially, sample standard deviation for Sharpe and all observations in the downside mean for Sortino. Undefined denominators produce missing labels, never infinity or the best rank. |
| Forward maximum drawdown and ulcer index | 7, 14, 21, 30, 45, 60, 90 days | Path-stability outcomes for shortlisted features and paper baskets. Include the entry value in each running-peak path. They test whether predicted low daily risk translates into a smoother equity curve; no separately optimised drawdown model in the first pass. |

The squared-risk specification clarifies the regression target; square root and annualisation are monotone, so it does not create extra univariate rank-IC tests. For each forecast horizon H, require at least `ceil(0.8*H)` fresh daily NAV endpoints, an observable entry NAV and at least two usable daily returns for variance. Retain genuinely observed unchanged NAVs and report adjacent observed-return counts. The required fresh-endpoint counts are 6, 12, 17, 24, 36, 48 and 72 for H = 7, 14, 21, 30, 45, 60 and 90 respectively. Otherwise mark the risk label unavailable. Keep the calendar grid: carried NAV days have zero marked returns and the observed catch-up change lands on the next fresh day, with its gap length recorded. This is a marked-return proxy, not evidence that the whole economic move occurred on that day. Never drop the intervening dates and annualise the surviving sequence as consecutive daily returns. Repeat on a fully observed subset and weekly intervals as measurement sensitivities.

No arbitrary extra label weights or thresholds.

### Why these targets fit the objective

The decision is “enough growth with less risk”, not “the highest forecast return” or “the smallest variance at any return”. A flat or steadily losing vault must not win solely on risk; an upward-jumping vault must not win solely on zero downside. Growth, downside semivariance and total variance expose these different cases. Daily risk is not path risk: returns with the same distribution can have different drawdowns when their order changes, hence the separate forward ulcer/drawdown checks.

The 20–30% annual portfolio objective is equivalent to approximately **1.51–2.18% compounded per 30 days**, before allowing for the differences between a vault's share-price return and the portfolio's net realised return. This is an economic reference, not a binary vault label or a promise every month will meet that range. Display `exp(365*g/elapsed_days)-1` for a realised window only as an annualisation. Exponentiating expected log growth does not give expected arithmetic return or an unbiased expected one-year CAGR; retain that distinction when calibrating entry thresholds.

The seven-horizon grid tests whether useful information concerns next-week deterioration, monthly quality or longer persistence; none is a literature-established optimum for these vaults. Rebalancing every two days does not require a two-day quality target: execution and exits remain governed by the simulator. Short horizons mature sooner but have fewer returns per risk label, more zero-downside cases and noisier ratios. Longer horizons provide richer paths but fewer independent temporal observations. Do not treat overlapping decisions or nested horizons as independent evidence.

A young vault with 30 days of history can receive a prediction immediately; the next H days, for H in the seven-horizon grid, are future labels, not an extra history requirement for live eligibility. Historical training rows become usable only after those outcomes are available. A 90-day label needs 90 future days, not a 90-day-old vault at entry.

For growth, report rank IC, forward return by score bucket, mean prediction versus realised mean growth, and improvement over production/age-only controls. For risk, compare with the trailing 30/90-day and EWM risk controls, showing calibration by forecast bucket, squared error on the variance scale and severe underprediction. For total variance also report QLIKE as `log(h) + y/h`, with a strictly positive forecast h and observed variance y; this form remains finite at y=0 and differs from the usual form only by observation-only terms where y>0. Report the fraction of zero-variance labels, forecasts at the floor, and QLIKE on positive-variance rows as a sensitivity; zero labels can otherwise make a floor-hugging model look useful. Freeze the prediction floor before validation. Do not log or floor genuine zero labels into invented losses. Risk-model point forecasts must be non-negative, with any transformation/back-transformation calibrated on training data only.

A model of `log(variance)` estimates a different quantity from expected variance; simply exponentiating it does not remove retransformation bias. Start on the untransformed squared-risk scale. Do not apply Patton's conditional-unbiased-proxy guarantee automatically to downside semivariance or our imperfect NAV labels. Confidence intervals and freshness sensitivities remain necessary. High rank IC without calibrated risk magnitudes is insufficient to justify inverse-risk position sizing.

Label clock: define the earliest feasible post-decision entry NAV and exit NAV at `entry_ts + H calendar days` under the recorded publication/execution convention. Key labels and predictions by `(vault_address, decision_ts, horizon_days, target)`; expose `entry_ts`, `label_end_ts`, `label_available_ts` and outcome status. Reuse feature rows across horizons. Missing 90-day outcomes must not delete valid 7-day outcomes, and every model may train only on its own fully matured labels. For gross share-price screens, state that actual redemption fees depend on position cost basis and are evaluated exactly in the portfolio simulator. Never subtract fees already internalised in share price.

Decision eligibility must not depend on whether a future label exists. Keep closure, failed redemption, missing endpoint and right-censoring states in the panel. Exclude genuinely unknown outcomes from numerical IC with counts; include known terminal losses. For unknown terminal NAVs, run two fixed scenarios: carry the last observed NAV to the horizon, and lose the full entry value. These are stress scenarios, not statistical bounds. Report the affected fraction and whether the shortlist changes; if it does, the feature remains unconfirmed. Missing intrahorizon risk paths remain unavailable, not reconstructed from those endpoint scenarios. Do not forward-fill a vanished vault indefinitely and label it safe. An executable portfolio must carry trapped holdings and capital, not delete them.

Its weighted-variance formula is specified below. Record warm-up and `n_eff = (sum weights)^2 / sum(weights^2)`. A span of 360 is a decay specification, not a claim of 360 days of data. Long-span values on a young vault may be computed after the common 30-day/coverage admission rule, but must be labelled partially warmed up and evaluated with their seed-weight/history diagnostics. - The broad pool initially needs 30 calendar days of observed history, at least 24 fresh daily NAV endpoints in the trailing 30 days, an observable opening NAV, and current tradability/capacity. - Add `has_feature`, actual duration and observation count for the few inputs used in a model. For linear models, impute using training-fold medians with missingness indicators; for univariate IC, report feature-specific coverage and paired common-row controls. ### Adaptive breadth without forced concentration

Replace “fill the top N” with “allocate across all candidates clearing an evidence/quality threshold, subject to risk and capacity”. Freeze a single economic entry hurdle from the 20% lower annual portfolio objective: `R_min(H) = 1.20**(H/365) - 1`. For a new position with external performance-fee fraction f and capital-redemption charge c, the approximate gross log-growth hurdle is `log1p((R_min(H)+c)/(1-f))`. Use the applicable fee mode so internalised fees are not subtracted again. This hurdle is stronger than merely covering exit costs; it is a conservative candidate admission policy, not a training label or a claim that every realised holding period must reach 20% annualised. Preserve a fee-only threshold as a labelled diagnostic if the stronger hurdle leaves negligible breadth. Calibrate the forecast scale using training/development out-of-fold predictions; require a positive calibration slope before interpreting its magnitude against the hurdle. If calibration is absent or unstable, the absolute-threshold allocator is unsupported rather than an excuse to fit a new cutoff to portfolio returns.
